# Hopper Day research

Reviewed September 7, 2026. These notes replace the original unsourced summary. The earlier working notes are no longer available. [research-audit.md](research-audit.md) records corrections, unresolved claims, and material left out of the page.

## What we are comparing

**Global AI-related capital investment against selected US federal budgets and public arts funding.** These are examples of scale chosen because their funding can be traced. They are not estimates of everything the world spends on medicine, animals, science, or art.

The AI baseline is **about $1 trillion for calendar 2026**, a forecast from Goldman Sachs Research published August 7, 2026. It estimates worldwide AI-related capital expenditure, adjusting hyperscaler spending for other companies, geography, and non-AI activity. It is neither venture funding alone nor an audited year-end total. [Goldman Sachs: Global AI Investment Is Forecast to Exceed $1 Trillion in 2026](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/global-investment-is-forecast-to-exceed-1-trillion-in-2026).

### Sources and definitions

We use the Animal Welfare line alone, not all animal protection spending. This is funding authority, not a measured expenditure total. ### Reading the ratios

This comparison puts private-sector investment across the world beside public funding in one country. Capital expenditure and government budget authority pay for different things. Fiscal years also differ from calendar years. FY2025 data are retained where the opened source provides a clear completed-year measure; they are not relabeled as 2026. No inflation adjustment is applied to the US table.

The ratios illustrate scale. They do not show that AI caused a particular budget cut, that these are interchangeable funding pools, or that spending more would produce proportionate results. They also do not establish that the listed programs are funded adequately. Those are separate questions.

## Windows, replacement, and e-waste

### What the estimates establish

PIRG estimated that **up to 400 million Windows 10 PCs could not upgrade to Windows 11** and associated that hardware with **up to 1.6 billion pounds of e-waste**. Attribute both figures to PIRG. Its updated page describes estimated waste, but does not establish that all those machines were physically discarded. We use the figure as the scale of hardware at risk, not a landfill count. [PIRG, Electronic Waste Graveyard](https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/electronic-waste-graveyard/); [PIRG's earlier explanation of the 400 million estimate](https://pirg.org/media-center/20000-call-on-microsoft-to-save-400-million-pcs/).

Windows 11 requirements include a supported processor, TPM 2.0, and UEFI firmware capable of Secure Boot. They are more than a TPM-chip purchase requirement. [Microsoft specifications](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/windows-11-specifications).

### The extension came after replacement pressure

- **October 9, 2025:** Omdia reported 72 million global PC shipments in Q3, up 6.8%, and identified the approaching Windows 10 deadline as the dominant driver of refresh demand. Its channel survey also showed unfinished migrations. These data include replacement and software-upgrade activity, not solely disposal. [Omdia's Q3 report](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/pr/2025/oct/omdia-global-pc-market-grew-7percent-in-q3-2025-as-windows-10-end-of-service-deadline-nears).
- **October 14, 2025:** Regular Windows 10 support ended. [Microsoft lifecycle announcement](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/announcements/windows-10-end-of-support).
- **June 25, 2026:** Windows Central reported the consumer ESU extension to October 2027. This dates the public reporting of the change, not the first existence of ESU. [Contemporaneous report](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/windows-10/microsoft-quietly-extends-windows-10s-extra-security-updates-program-for-free-users-can-now-stay-on-windows-10-until-october-2027-securely).
- **June 30, 2026:** Omdia described a Q1 US demand slowdown after a Windows 11 refresh cycle that had exhausted much of the near-term commercial pipeline. [Omdia's Q1 report](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/pr/2026/june/us-pc-shipments-fell-7percent-in-1q26-marking-steepest-decline-since-2023).
- **At this review:** Microsoft's consumer support page gives **October 12, 2027** as the ESU endpoint. Some other Microsoft pages still display October 2026. Use the current consumer page and date any statement about the extension. [Microsoft consumer support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/deployment/updates-lifecycle/windows-10-support-has-ended-on-october-14-2025).

**Interpretation:** The later consumer extension buys time for remaining machines. It cannot reverse purchases and fleet decisions already made around the original deadline. The evidence supports a substantial refresh wave, particularly in commercial fleets. It does not establish that most of the original 400 million PCs belonged to SMBs, schools, and enterprises, or that most have now been replaced or discarded. Education and SMB completion rates remain unverified.

Consumer ESU is separate from organizational ESU. Microsoft's organizational documentation already described up to three years of updates for enrolled commercial and educational devices in November 2025. It lists standard business licensing at $61 per device in year one, doubling annually; do not apply that price to education or every licensing arrangement. [Microsoft organizational ESU documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/whats-new/extended-security-updates).

## Hopper, the date, and Omarchy

Hopper and her team developed early compilers and FLOW-MATIC, which influenced COBOL. The connection to today's conversational tools is our interpretation of that history, not a claim that she foresaw a specific product or that programming accessibility is solved. [Computer History Museum, Grace Hopper oral history, 1980, abstract on PDF p. 3](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/text/Oral_History/Hopper_Grace/102702026.05.01.pdf#page=3).

The [Omarchy v4.0.0 release](https://github.com/omacom/omarchy/releases/tag/v4.0.0) is dated August 14, 2026. This project chooses that anniversary for Hopper Day. It does not claim August 14 marks the invention of conversational computing, Hopper's birthday, or an officially recognized holiday.

The [current installation guide](https://omarchy.org/manual/getting-started/) describes an ISO/USB installation and says the installation stage can take under a minute on fast machines and up to roughly five minutes on older ones. That is project guidance, not our benchmark or an end-to-end guarantee. Downloading, preparing media, configuring firmware, and setting up an agent add steps. Hardware compatibility must be checked; the absence of Windows 11 eligibility does not itself prove Omarchy compatibility.

The [AI chapter](https://omarchy.org/manual/ai/) describes optional launchers that download agent tools on first use and require authentication. The sample “hey hopper” requests on our page are illustrative, not commands for an included assistant called Hopper. An older computer can be the client for a cloud agent; that does not mean it runs the model locally. Account, connectivity, and service costs depend on the tool chosen.

## Keeping this research usable

A new statistic needs a direct link, a period, geography, units, and a description of what is counted. Mark forecasts, estimates, requests, appropriations, obligations, and outlays separately. Recalculate ratios from the source amounts. Preserve caveats that change the argument, including evidence of reuse and extended support. PRs correcting sources or improving these definitions are welcome.
